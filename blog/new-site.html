site redesign

July 31, 2026 at 00:32

Home Blog

I figured my old site was pretty outdated, and boring. It had some charm I guess, but I just wanted something fresh. I went for a classic MacOS Aqua-ish theme. There is a lot of charm in old software design and looking back at them is pretty cool.

Why Mac OS X?

The early 2000s were certainly a wierd time for design. A lot of interfaces (like the iPhone) were all skeumorphic. I do think Mac OS X nailed the aqua theme, I love the brushed metal, and the aqua buttons. The fake shadows were pretty cool too, and there was definitely a lot of love put into the designs.

A lot of modern web design, well, software design in general, feels very sterile now. Especially with generative ai, things just... stick out. They just don't feel right. I wanted to base my design around something that has a lot of personality, which Mac OS X definitly has.

What I changed

I tried to keep things simple. I added the rounded buttons. Semi-transparent cards, some dithering in the background. Oh and I can't forget the font either, I used Lucida Grande, the same font used in the era of skeumorphic Mac OS X. It definitly dates the site, but honestly thats the point. I just like something simple, so I made something simple.

Oh and I also consolidated my blog stuff into one unified area. I don't know why, but I decided to split notes and regular posts across two separate sections? I dunno, I never wrote anything anyways so its not like it made that big of a difference either. At least now everything is in one main blog section. Theres also a projects page too, which at the current time of writing, I haven't actually published yet.. but I just want a place where I can ramble (and brag) about random software I've made instead of having to shove it all on the homepage. That also means I can keep my more current stuff on the homepage, and rotate depending on what it is I'm currently working on.

I am pretty proud that I was able to build all this without any JavaScript. Just good ol static HTML and CSS. I hate writing JS, I hate vendoring JS packages. I think people just waste time abstracting away all the fun stuff, and they honestly suck the fun out of building something with html.

Why it matters

I truly believe your portfolio should show you as a person. Who you are, what you care about. I want mine to feel like it was built with love, from the ground up, instead of offloading everything to some AI Agent so you can claim you "made my portfolio". I work on some low level shit, reverse engineering, things that honestly feel like they're built to last if that makes sense. I just want my site to reflect that...

I do strongly believe old software design has a quality that has been lost to time. I think it looked pretty good without trying too hard either. I wanted to capture some of that feeling here.

I do still believe I suck at web dev, but at least I can say I tried my best, and built it myself.
